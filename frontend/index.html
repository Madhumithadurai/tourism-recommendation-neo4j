How the algorithms work
  • BFS expands towns in order of legs travelled: fewest stops, ignores hours.
  • Dijkstra always expands the cheapest town so far: optimal for time, distance or cost.
  • A* adds a straight-line estimate to the goal (never an over-estimate), so it expands fewer towns and is still optimal.
  • Greedy follows only the estimate: very fast, sometimes a worse route.
  • Verify in Neo4j runs the same question as Cypher on the database, with your closures and traffic as parameters.

What the numbers mean
  • Hit@5 / Hit@10: share of tourists whose hidden visit is in the top 5 / 10.
  • MRR: 1 / rank of the hidden visit, averaged (higher is better).
  • The popularity baseline ignores the tourist. Collaborative filtering needs many similar tourists; with 20 it is limited (sparsity).
